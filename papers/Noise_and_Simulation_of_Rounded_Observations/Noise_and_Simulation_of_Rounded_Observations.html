Noise and Simulation of Rounded Observations

Exact scalar classification and the cost of preserving dependencies

Samir Bouftass

15 November 2026 · English edition 1.1

Abstract

We study simulation from one already rounded observation of a noisy scalar state. For P=LQP=LQ, the observation is Y=⌊[Z+E]P/Q⌋Y=\lfloor[Z+E]_P/Q\rfloor, and the target is W=⌊[c(Z+E)+b]P/Q⌋W=\lfloor[c(Z+E)+b]_P/Q\rfloor. The noise takes values −1,0,1-1,0,1 with probabilities η,1−2η,η\eta,1-2\eta,\eta, where 0≤η≤1/40\le\eta\le1/4. We classify every stochastic kernel from YY that reproduces the target channel for all states. Except at the maximal noise level, only constant maps and aligned translations or reflections are possible. At η=1/4\eta=1/4 and even PP, multiplication by P/2P/2 adds one exceptional family: a state-independent fair coin. The exception comes from the alternating mode in the kernel of convolution. For even QQ, we determine both the optimal error below this noise level and the positive error that remains when the original rounded observation must also be preserved. Explicit finite certificates separate simulation with access to the raw noisy value from simulation based only on its rounded quotient. The results concern a fixed scalar information constraint, not a general impossibility of cryptographic reductions.

1. Model and simulation requirement

Write [x]P[x]_P for the representative in {0,…,P−1}\{0,\ldots,P-1\}. Fix P=LQP=LQ with L,Q≥2L,Q\ge2, and define F(x)=⌊[x]P/Q⌋F(x)=\lfloor[x]_P/Q\rfloor. A hidden state Z∈ℤPZ\in\mathbb Z_P is perturbed by independent noise

Pr(E=−1)=Pr(E=1)=η,Pr(E=0)=1−2η,0≤η≤14. \Pr(E=-1)=\Pr(E=1)=\eta,\qquad \Pr(E=0)=1-2\eta, \quad 0\le\eta\le\tfrac14. \tag{1}

Let X=[Z+E]PX=[Z+E]_P, Y=F(X)Y=F(X), and W=h(X)W=h(X), where h(x)=F(cx+b)h(x)=F(cx+b) and c,b∈ℤPc,b\in\mathbb Z_P. A simulator receives one value YY and samples an output using a row-stochastic matrix K(y,w)K(y,w). Its randomness is fresh, and it receives neither ZZ nor the discarded remainder. The same kernel must satisfy

ℒ(K(Y)∣Z=z)=ℒ(W∣Z=z)for every z∈ℤP. \mathcal L(K(Y)\mid Z=z)=\mathcal L(W\mid Z=z) \qquad\text{for every }z\in\mathbb Z_P. \tag{2}

Equivalently, it preserves the joint law of (Z,W)(Z,W) for every prior on ZZ. This requirement does not include preserving (Z,Y,W)(Z,Y,W), which is a stronger task considered in Section 6. There is no rejection, additional observation, or change of parameters in (2).

The target uses the same noisy value as the source. In particular, its noise before rounding is cEcE, not a fresh copy of EE added after cZcZ. Substituting fresh noise would define a different channel.

An elementary secret-based interpretation is available without importing any hardness assumption. Suppose a public AA is a unit modulo PP, S∈ℤPS\in\mathbb Z_P, and Z=ASZ=AS. For each fixed AA, multiplication by AA is a bijection. Thus statewise simulation is equivalent to preserving (A,S,W)(A,S,W) for all secret priors, allowing a kernel depending on AA. This observation does not extend the theorem to nonunits, restricted secret supports, or dense matrix problems.

Let Hx,y=𝟏{F(x)=y}H_{x,y}=\mathbf1\{F(x)=y\} and Gx,w=𝟏{h(x)=w}G_{x,w}=\mathbf1\{h(x)=w\}. Define the cyclic convolution operator

3. A scalar factorization lemma

The following elementary statement provides all deterministic cases.

Lemma 2. The function x↦F(cx+b)x\mapsto F(cx+b) is constant on every cell of FF if and only if one of the following holds: c=0c=0; c=1c=1 and b≡0(modQ)b\equiv0\pmod Q; or c=−1c=-1 and b≡Q−1(modQ)b\equiv Q-1\pmod Q.

Proof. Write x=Qy+rx=Qy+r, 0≤r<Q0\le r<Q. Then

F(cx+b)=[cy+⌊cr+bQ⌋]L. F(cx+b)=\left[cy+\left\lfloor\frac{cr+b}{Q}\right\rfloor\right]_L. \tag{5}

It suffices to study the first cell. If c≠0c\ne0, put d=gcd(c,P)d=\gcd(c,P) and ℓ=P/d\ell=P/d. When ℓ<Q\ell<Q, the QQ terms [b+cr]P[b+cr]_P contain a full subgroup coset, whose canonical representatives have diameter P−d≥P/2≥QP-d\ge P/2\ge Q, preventing containment in a length-QQ interval. When ℓ=Q\ell=Q, the QQ distinct terms occupy all QQ points of the cell, so the presence of consecutive points forces d=1d=1, a contradiction. When ℓ>Q\ell>Q, the QQ distinct terms occupy all QQ points of the cell and again force d=1d=1. Shifting the progression by cc gives an overlap of Q−1Q-1 points with that interval. For a length-QQ interval II,

|I∩(I+c)|=max(0,Q−[c]P)+max(0,Q−(P−[c]P)). |I\cap(I+c)|=\max(0,Q-[c]_P)+\max(0,Q-(P-[c]_P)).

Since Q≤P/2Q\le P/2, equality with Q−1Q-1 forces c=1c=1 or c=−1c=-1. The first or last point must align with a cell endpoint, giving the two congruences. Formula (5) proves sufficiency, and the case c=0c=0 is immediate. ▫\square

Proof. If CηC_\eta is invertible, (3) implies HK=GHK=G. Thus hh is constant on each cell, and Lemma 2 applies.

In the singular case, there is a row vector aa such that

K(F(x),⋅)−δh(x)=(−1)xa. K(F(x),\cdot)-\delta_{h(x)}=(-1)^x a. \tag{6}

If a=0a=0, Lemma 2 again gives the deterministic cases. Suppose a≠0a\ne0. Each cell contains both parities. Averaging the two equations in (6) gives K(y,⋅)=(δu+δv)/2K(y,\cdot)=(\delta_u+\delta_v)/2 for every yy.

The images of all even inputs under x↦cx+bx\mapsto cx+b form the coset b+⟨2c⟩b+\langle2c\rangle, contained in one rounding cell. A nontrivial subgroup coset has representative span at least P/2≥QP/2\ge Q, which is impossible. Thus 2c=02c=0 modulo PP. Since c=0c=0 would make u=vu=v, necessarily c=P/2c=P/2.

Conversely, at η=1/4\eta=1/4, the parity of Z+EZ+E is fair for every ZZ. Multiplication by P/2P/2 depends only on this parity, giving the equal mixture in the table. The two output values are distinct because points separated by P/2≥QP/2\ge Q cannot lie in one cell. Uniqueness follows from Lemma 1. ▫\square

The exceptional kernel erases state information. Its existence is not evidence of a hard learning problem: its target distribution is independent of ZZ.

5. Exact error below the exceptional noise level

Assume now that QQ is even, so P=LQP=LQ is even, and let c=P/2c=P/2. Put α=F(b)\alpha=F(b), β=F(b+P/2)\beta=F(b+P/2), and d=1−4ηd=1-4\eta. For each state,

tz=Pr(W=α∣Z=z)=1+d(−1)z2. t_z=\Pr(W=\alpha\mid Z=z)=\frac{1+d(-1)^z}{2}.

6. Preserving the original observation

Let ZZ be uniform, η=1/4\eta=1/4, QQ even, and c=P/2c=P/2. Exactness for (Z,W)(Z,W) does not imply exactness for (Z,Y,W)(Z,Y,W).

Theorem 3. Among all fresh YY-only kernels,

infKΔ(ℒ(Z,Y,W),ℒ(Z,Y,K(Y)))=12Q. \inf_K\Delta\bigl(\mathcal L(Z,Y,W),\mathcal L(Z,Y,K(Y))\bigr)=\frac1{2Q}. \tag{7}

The constant fair coin attains this value and also reproduces the public law of (Y,W)(Y,W) exactly.

Proof. Mapping every output outside {α,β}\{\alpha,\beta\} to α\alpha fixes the real distribution and cannot increase total variation. We may therefore describe a kernel by py=K(y,α)p_y=K(y,\alpha). For a fixed observed cell yy, reflect

z↦[(2y+1)Q−1−z]P,E↦−E. z\longmapsto[(2y+1)Q-1-z]_P,\qquad E\longmapsto-E.

This preserves the conditional law given Y=yY=y and swaps the parity of XX, hence swaps α\alpha and β\beta. The contribution of cell yy to the objective is convex in pyp_y and invariant under py↦1−pyp_y\mapsto1-p_y. It is minimized at py=1/2p_y=1/2.

For an interior state, whose residue modulo QQ is in {1,…,Q−2}\{1,\ldots,Q-2\}, the rounded cell is fixed and the target is fair, so the conditional error is zero. At either boundary state of a cell, the outward noise step has mass 1/41/4 and a deterministic target; replacing it by a fair coin contributes 1/81/8 to total variation. Within the original cell, the two target masses are 1/2,1/41/2,1/4, replaced by 3/8,3/83/8,3/8, contributing another 1/81/8. Thus each boundary state contributes 1/41/4. Boundary states constitute a fraction 2/Q2/Q of all states, yielding (7), including Q=2Q=2.

Finally, XX is uniform, and each even-length cell contains equally many values of either parity. Hence (Y,W)(Y,W) is exactly a uniform quotient paired with an independent fair coin. ▫\square

For example, with P=8P=8, Q=2Q=2, b=0b=0, and z=0z=0, the real masses on (Y,W)(Y,W) are (3,2):1/4(3,2):1/4, (0,0):1/2(0,0):1/2, and (0,2):1/4(0,2):1/4. The simulated masses are 1/81/8 at each of (3,0),(3,2)(3,0),(3,2) and 3/83/8 at each of (0,0),(0,2)(0,0),(0,2), at distance 1/41/4.

The optimum depends on the view being compared:

(Y,W)(Y,W) 00

The last row follows because WW is deterministic given XX, while each cell contains equal proportions of the two possible targets. A YY-only prediction is therefore wrong with probability at least 1/21/2.

7. Comparison with output-dependent error models

Learning with Errors with Output Dependencies (LWE-OD) permits the error distribution to depend on the underlying inner product [2]. Any finite target channel NN in this paper can be encoded in that language. Extend Nz,wN_{z,w} by zero outside 0≤w<L0\le w<L, and define an error law on ℤP\mathbb Z_P by

ϕz(e)=Nz,[z+e]P. \phi_z(e)=N_{z,[z+e]_P}. \tag{8}

Then adding this error modulo PP gives exactly the output channel NN. The same construction applies to MM. Expressibility as an output-dependent error model does not, however, settle which already coarsened source can simulate the channel.

Hoffmann et al. [2] give reductions through an intermediate problem called qLWE. Their reductions assume, in particular, that the modulus qq is polynomial in the dimension nn and that the secret has at least one coordinate invertible modulo qq. Their Algorithm 1 uses rejection, and their Algorithm 2 and Proposition 5 use additional independent noise on unrounded LWE samples. Their sufficient Fourier conditions in Theorems 2–3 include both spectral support and nonnegativity constraints. In particular, they already account for zeros of the noise spectrum. Our exceptional alternating mode is not a new general treatment of spectral zeros. The extra constraint here is that the simulator acts through the fixed quotient matrix HH in (3).

Two exact certificates make this distinction concrete. They use the symmetric noise law ψ\psi from (1). For a positive output mass, write

ρ(w)=1P∑zNz,w,ξw(j)=N[w−j]P,wPρ(w). \rho(w)=\frac1P\sum_zN_{z,w},\qquad \xi_w(j)=\frac{N_{[w-j]_P,w}}{P\rho(w)}. \tag{9}

At inactive outputs, a conditional law may be chosen freely, as in Remark 1 of [2]. A probability distribution τw\tau_w satisfying τw*ψ=ξw\tau_w*\psi=\xi_w supplies an explicit convolution certificate for the corresponding sufficient condition in [2].

Raw information can suffice when the quotient does not. Take P=8P=8, Q=2Q=2, c=2c=2, b=0b=0, and η=1/8\eta=1/8. Then h(x)=[x]4h(x)=[x]_4, the active outputs have ρ(w)=1/4\rho(w)=1/4, and each active ξw\xi_w equals ψ*τ\psi*\tau with τ=(δ0+δ4)/2\tau=(\delta_0+\delta_4)/2. Thus a nonnegative convolution certificate exists. Direct access to XX also plainly suffices to compute h(X)h(X). Yet no kernel from YY exists: convolution is invertible, while F(0)=F(1)F(0)=F(1) and h(0)≠h(1)h(0)\ne h(1), so HK=GHK=G is impossible. Exact elimination gives rank(M)=4\operatorname{rank}(M)=4 and rank([M∣N⋅,0])=5\operatorname{rank}([M\mid N_{\cdot,0}])=5. This is a separation between source information models, not a counterexample to [2].

The singular case also fits the earlier framework. For P=8P=8, Q=2Q=2, c=4c=4, b=0b=0, and η=1/4\eta=1/4, every target row is the fair distribution on {0,2}\{0,2\}. Both active conditional laws ξw\xi_w are uniform on ℤ8\mathbb Z_8. Taking τw\tau_w uniform gives τw*ψ=ξw\tau_w*\psi=\xi_w, despite the spectral zero at frequency 44. The quotient kernel is also exact for (Z,W)(Z,W), but Theorem 3 still gives error 1/41/4 for (Z,Y,W)(Z,Y,W).

The relevant distinction is therefore an exhaustive classification under the already rounded, single-observation constraint, together with the distances for specified views. No general LWE-OD hardness statement is inferred from these finite examples. The asymptotic hypotheses and access models of the reductions in [2] remain separate.

8. Cryptographic scope

LWR provides an important motivation for studying rounded observations [3,4], but the scalar theorems here neither prove a cryptographic reduction nor rule out broader ones. They exclude changes of secrets, dimensions, moduli, sample counts, and information access; computational rather than statistical simulation is also outside the model.

There is a further obstacle to interpreting the exceptional fair coin as a reduction. In the binary-modulus setting P=2pP=2^p, Q=2qQ=2^q, its two outputs differ by L/2L/2. If the null target is uniform on ℤL\mathbb Z_L, this two-point distribution is not the null law when L>2L>2. If L=2L=2, both real and null targets are uniform and the resulting target problem is trivial. The aligned translations and reflections permute quotient values and preserve a uniform null law, but this fact alone says nothing about structured matrix distributions.

More generally, if a transformation has errors ε0,ε1\varepsilon_0,\varepsilon_1 in the two worlds of a distinguishing experiment, the triangle inequality gives the acceptance-gap bound

Advsource≥Advtarget−ε0−ε1. \operatorname{Adv}_{\mathrm{source}}\ge \operatorname{Adv}_{\mathrm{target}}-\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon_1. \tag{10}

Exact simulation of only the real marginal leaves the second error uncontrolled.

9. Reproducible verification

The supplement supplies three independent checks. The exact verifier constructs the channels directly using fractions.Fraction, selects independent source rows, solves for the unique candidate kernel, and checks all equations and stochastic constraints. It evaluates 8,049 coefficient-and-translation cases for 4≤P≤164\le P\le16, all proper divisors Q≥2Q\ge2, and η∈{0,1/8,1/4}\eta\in\{0,1/8,1/4\}. The 1,235 feasible cases consist of 645 constants, 207 positive maps, 207 reflections, and 176 parity cases. All 6,814 excluded cases fail exact linear consistency. There are 57 full-column-rank checks.

The distance calculations cover 34 even-divisor pairs with P≤32P\le32. Exact piecewise-linear optimization checks the stronger-view distance; 280 unit-multiplication checks verify the elementary secret reparameterization. A separate numerical implementation solves 72 linear programs for the average and worst-state errors, with tolerance 10−910^{-9}. Finally, a third program verifies the two convolution and rank certificates in Section 7 without importing the classification. Negative controls detect fresh target noise, omitted reflection translations, subcritical parity bias, and the null-world mismatch. The delivered results identify their exact or floating-point arithmetic; only the mathematical proofs justify the unrestricted theorems.
