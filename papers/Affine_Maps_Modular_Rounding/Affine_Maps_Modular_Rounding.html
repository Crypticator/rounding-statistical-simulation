Affine Maps Compatible with Modular Rounding

Exact factorization, missing carries, and prediction loss

Samir Bouftass and ChatGPT (OpenAI)

15 November 2026 · English edition 1.1

Abstract

Let P=KQP=KQ with K,Q≥2K,Q\ge2, and let F(z)=⌊[z]P/Q⌋F(z)=\lfloor[z]_P/Q\rfloor act coordinatewise. We classify all affine maps for which F(Uz+b)F(Uz+b) can be computed from F(z)F(z) for every input. Each row of UU, reduced modulo PP, must be zero or contain exactly one nonzero entry, equal to 11 or −1-1. Positive and negative rows require different congruences on the translation. In particular, an invertible square linear map compatible with this rounding is a permutation matrix; allowing an appropriate translation enlarges the class to signed permutations. We also quantify the information lost in a two-coordinate sum. Its optimal prediction error from the rounded inputs is (Q−1)/(2Q)(Q-1)/(2Q). Resampling the missing carry reproduces the public marginal exactly, but generally fails to preserve its dependence on the unrounded input or a hidden secret. The contribution is an explicit normal form for a fixed interval partition and elementary, sharp examples of the distinction between public simulation and joint simulation. The general principles of factorization through fibers and optimal finite prediction are standard.

1. Question and setting

Rounding occurs in lattice-based cryptography, including Learning with Rounding and its variants [1–5]. A linear operation on an unrounded value need not remain computable after rounding: the operation may depend on a remainder that has already been discarded. The question considered here is exact and algebraic. For which public affine maps does there exist a function gg such that

F(Uz+b)=g(F(z))for every z∈ℤPm? F(Uz+b)=g(F(z))\qquad\text{for every }z\in\mathbb Z_P^m? \tag{1}

We write [x]P[x]_P for the representative of xx in {0,…,P−1}\{0,\ldots,P-1\}. Fix integers P=KQP=KQ, K,Q≥2K,Q\ge2, and positive dimensions m,nm,n. The map F:ℤPm→ℤKmF:\mathbb Z_P^m\to\mathbb Z_K^m is applied coordinatewise. The matrix UU has size n×mn\times m, and bb has length nn; both have integer entries interpreted modulo PP. No linearity, efficiency, or other restriction is imposed on gg.

Equation (1) is a universal identity. It differs from an identity valid only on a special input support, from a probabilistic approximation, and from a cryptographic reduction that changes dimensions, secrets, noise, or moduli. We make these distinctions explicit after proving the classification.

The fibers of FF form a fixed partition into products of consecutive intervals. Maps preserving a partition are classical objects [6]; congruence-preserving maps concern a related but different kind of partition [7]. Our result computes the affine maps compatible with this particular interval partition. It does not introduce the general fiber-factorization criterion.

2. The exact carry identity

For each input, write

z=Qy+r+Ph,y=F(z),0≤rj<Q, z=Qy+r+Ph,\qquad y=F(z),\qquad 0\le r_j<Q,

where hh is an integer vector if an arbitrary integer representative of zz is used. Euclidean division gives

F(Uz+b)=[Uy+⌊Ur+bQ⌋]K. F(Uz+b)=\left[Uy+\left\lfloor\frac{Ur+b}{Q}\right\rfloor\right]_K. \tag{2}

Indeed, adding a multiple of P=KQP=KQ changes the integer quotient by a multiple of KK. The remaining multiple QUyQUy can be taken outside the floor. This proves (2), including negative coefficients, negative translations, and modular wraparound. Floors and reductions in this formula act coordinatewise.

The correction term depends on the discarded remainder rr. Consequently, (1) holds if and only if F(Ur+b)F(Ur+b) is constant over the zero cell {0,…,Q−1}m\{0,\ldots,Q-1\}^m. Necessity follows by taking y=0y=0. For sufficiency, (2) then gives the same correction for every yy.

3. Complete affine classification

Lemma 1 (a progression contained in one cell). Suppose a≠0a\ne0 in ℤP\mathbb Z_P. If the QQ points [t+ar]P[t+ar]_P, 0≤r<Q0\le r<Q, lie in one aligned interval of length QQ, then either a=1a=1 and t≡0(modQ)t\equiv0\pmod Q, or a=−1a=-1 and t≡Q−1(modQ)t\equiv Q-1\pmod Q.

Proof. Set d=gcd(a,P)d=\gcd(a,P) and ℓ=P/d\ell=P/d, the order of aa. If ℓ<Q\ell<Q, the progression contains the entire coset of the subgroup generated by aa. Its representatives have span P−d≥P/2≥QP-d\ge P/2\ge Q, so they cannot fit in an interval of span Q−1Q-1. If ℓ=Q\ell=Q, the QQ distinct points fill the interval. Since all have one residue modulo dd, two consecutive points force d=1d=1, contradicting ℓ=Q<P\ell=Q<P.

Thus ℓ>Q\ell>Q. The QQ points again fill the interval, forcing d=1d=1. Write this interval as CC. The progressions indexed by 0,…,Q−10,\ldots,Q-1 and 1,…,Q1,\ldots,Q have exactly Q−1Q-1 common points, so |C∩(C+a)|=Q−1|C\cap(C+a)|=Q-1. For 1≤a<P1\le a<P and Q≤P/2Q\le P/2,

|C∩(C+a)|=max(0,Q−a)+max(0,Q−(P−a)). |C\cap(C+a)|=\max(0,Q-a)+\max(0,Q-(P-a)).

At most one term is positive. Equality with Q−1Q-1 therefore forces a=1a=1 or a=P−1a=P-1. Alignment of the first or last point gives the stated congruence on tt. ▫\square

Theorem 1 (normal form). The following conditions are equivalent:

  1. There exists gg satisfying (1).
  2. The map r↦F(Ur+b)r\mapsto F(Ur+b) is constant on {0,…,Q−1}m\{0,\ldots,Q-1\}^m.
  3. Every row of UU, modulo PP, has one of the forms in the table below, with the indicated condition on bib_i.
Row of UU Translation condition Output coordinate gi(y)g_i(y) in ℤK\mathbb Z_K
Zero Any bib_i ⌊[bi]P/Q⌋\lfloor[b_i]_P/Q\rfloor
One entry +1+1 in column jj bi≡0(modQ)b_i\equiv0\pmod Q yj+⌊[bi]P/Q⌋y_j+\lfloor[b_i]_P/Q\rfloor
One entry −1-1 in column jj bi≡Q−1(modQ)b_i\equiv Q-1\pmod Q −yj+⌊[bi]P/Q⌋-y_j+\lfloor[b_i]_P/Q\rfloor

Different rows may use the same column. Whenever it exists, gg is unique.

Proof. The equivalence of the first two conditions follows from (2). Consider a row satisfying the second condition. Varying one remainder coordinate while fixing the others at zero and applying Lemma 1 shows that every nonzero coefficient is 11 or −1-1, with the corresponding congruence on the row translation. A row cannot contain both signs, because this would require bi≡0b_i\equiv0 and bi≡Q−1(modQ)b_i\equiv Q-1\pmod Q, which are distinct for Q≥2Q\ge2.

If two coefficients are 11, the translation is QβQ\beta. Setting both corresponding remainders to Q−1Q-1 changes the correction quotient from β\beta to β+1\beta+1. If two coefficients are −1-1, write the translation as Qβ+Q−1Q\beta+Q-1. The same choice changes the correction from β\beta to β−1\beta-1. These changes are nonzero modulo K≥2K\ge2, contradicting constancy. Thus at most one coefficient is nonzero. Substitution into (2) proves sufficiency and the formulas in the table. Finally, FF is surjective, which gives uniqueness. ▫\square

Corollary 1. With b=0b=0, each row is zero or selects one coordinate with coefficient 11. If UU is square and invertible over ℤP\mathbb Z_P, it is a permutation matrix. With unrestricted admissible translations, square invertible matrices are exactly signed permutation matrices.

For the last assertion, invertibility rules out zero rows and repeated selected columns. Conversely, signed permutations are invertible, and the prescribed translations make their rows admissible. The classification concerns coefficients modulo PP, not merely modulo the smaller output modulus KK.

4. Examples and support restrictions

An invertible shear fails. Take P=4P=4, Q=2Q=2, and the map (z1,z2)↦(z1+z2,z2)(z_1,z_2)\mapsto(z_1+z_2,z_2). Inputs (0,0)(0,0) and (1,1)(1,1) have the same rounded value (0,0)(0,0). Their transformed rounded values are (0,0)(0,0) and (1,0)(1,0). Thus invertibility before rounding does not imply computability after rounding.

A corrected reflection succeeds. For every zz,

F(−z−1)=K−1−F(z). F(-z-1)=K-1-F(z). \tag{3}

The translation is essential. Unshifted negation does not, in general, preserve the rounding cells. More generally, translations by multiples of the quantization step commute with the quotient in the familiar way; related translation identities also appear in modulo quantization [8].

Restricted supports can change the answer. If every coordinate of zz is a multiple of QQ, all remainder coordinates vanish, and every integer matrix satisfies F(Uz)=[UF(z)]KF(Uz)=[UF(z)]_K. This does not contradict Theorem 1, whose hypothesis ranges over all inputs.

For an arbitrary subset Ω⊆ℤPm\Omega\subseteq\mathbb Z_P^m, a factorization exists on Ω\Omega exactly when F(Uz+b)F(Uz+b) is constant on each nonempty set Ω∩F−1(y)\Omega\cap F^{-1}(y). Necessity is immediate; for sufficiency, define g(y)g(y) by that constant and choose arbitrary values on unused fibers. This is a set-theoretic criterion and supplies no efficient algorithm by itself.

5. Optimal prediction of a missing carry

Let ZZ have a specified finite distribution, and set Y=F(Z)Y=F(Z) and T=F(UZ+b)T=F(UZ+b). A simulator receives only YY and uses fresh randomness, independent of ZZ conditional on YY. Its smallest disagreement probability is

ε*=1−∑ymaxtPr(Y=y,T=t). \varepsilon_* =1-\sum_y\max_t\Pr(Y=y,T=t). \tag{4}

For a kernel K(y,t)K(y,t), success is ∑y,tPr(Y=y,T=t)K(y,t)\sum_{y,t}\Pr(Y=y,T=t)K(y,t). Maximizing each row of this linear expression puts all its mass on a most likely target value. This proves (4). The optimum is information-theoretic; computing the required conditional probabilities may be difficult.

Now let Z1,Z2Z_1,Z_2 be independent and uniform on ℤP\mathbb Z_P, and set Y=(F(Z1),F(Z2))Y=(F(Z_1),F(Z_2)) and T=F(Z1+Z2)T=F(Z_1+Z_2). The remainders R1,R2R_1,R_2 are independent uniform elements of {0,…,Q−1}\{0,\ldots,Q-1\}, independent of the quotients. The missing carry C=⌊(R1+R2)/Q⌋C=\lfloor(R_1+R_2)/Q\rfloor is binary, with

p0=Pr(C=0)=Q+12Q,p1=Pr(C=1)=Q−12Q. p_0=\Pr(C=0)=\frac{Q+1}{2Q},\qquad p_1=\Pr(C=1)=\frac{Q-1}{2Q}. \tag{5}

There are Q(Q+1)/2Q(Q+1)/2 pairs whose sum is below QQ. The two possible output values differ modulo K≥2K\ge2. Hence

ε*=Q−12Q. \varepsilon_* =\frac{Q-1}{2Q}. \tag{6}

The same optimum holds for the entire shear output, since its second quotient is already known.

6. Public simulation and joint simulation

We use total variation in the convention Δ(μ,ν)=12∑x|μ(x)−ν(x)|\Delta(\mu,\nu)=\tfrac12\sum_x|\mu(x)-\nu(x)|. In the preceding example, independently drawing a new carry with probabilities (p0,p1)(p_0,p_1) reproduces the law of (Y,T)(Y,T) exactly. It need not reproduce the dependence of TT on the original unrounded input.

Whenever TT is deterministic given ZZ and YY is a function of ZZ, a fresh YY-only simulator satisfies

Δ(ℒ(Z,T),ℒ(Z,T̃))=Pr(T≠T̃). \Delta\bigl(\mathcal L(Z,T),\mathcal L(Z,\widetilde T)\bigr) =\Pr(T\ne\widetilde T). \tag{7}

To prove this, condition on each zz: the distance from the point mass at T(z)T(z) to the simulated conditional distribution is 1−Pr(T̃=T(z)∣z)1-\Pr(\widetilde T=T(z)\mid z). Averaging proves the identity. For independent posterior resampling of the carry, (7) gives

2p0p1=Q2−12Q2, 2p_0p_1=\frac{Q^2-1}{2Q^2}, \tag{8}

whereas the smallest joint distance is p1p_1, obtained by always predicting the more likely carry. Exactness of a public marginal and optimality for a stronger joint view are therefore different objectives.

A binary-secret example makes the distinction especially transparent. Let P=4P=4, Q=2Q=2, take the public column A=(1,1)𝖳A=(1,1)^\mathsf{T}, a uniform bit SS, and no noise. The observed vector Y=F(AS)Y=F(AS) is always (0,0)(0,0). Under the shear (z1,z2)↦(z1+z2,z2)(z_1,z_2)\mapsto(z_1+z_2,z_2), the target is T=(S,0)T=(S,0). Every simulator using only (A,Y)(A,Y) is independent of SS, so its distance from the joint law of (S,T)(S,T) is at least 1/21/2. Equality is attained by every simulator supported on (0,0)(0,0) and (1,0)(1,0); an equal mixture also reproduces the public target marginal exactly. No choice of fresh public randomness reconstructs the missing correlation with SS.

These examples do not exclude more general reductions with additional information or altered distributions. They identify a specific information constraint: Z→Y→T̃Z\to Y\to\widetilde T is a Markov chain, while the real target can retain information about ZZ beyond YY.

7. Relation to earlier work and scope

Partition-preserving transformation semigroups provide the general language for condition (1) [6]. The normal form above computes the intersection of affine maps with transformations preserving a fixed product of interval cells. Congruence-preserving functions [7] are defined using algebraic congruences. Our cells are not cosets of a proper subgroup: a cell containing both 00 and 11 cannot be such a coset. For example, multiplication by 22 modulo 44 preserves group congruences, but separates 00 and 11 in the same rounding cell for Q=2Q=2. Even translation by 11 can split a cell. These differences prevent an automatic transfer of classifications for subgroup partitions to this setting.

The LWE and LWR literature [1–5] supplies the cryptographic motivation, not a hardness consequence of Theorem 1. The theorem concerns universal exact postprocessing at fixed parameters. It does not cover unequal cells when QQ does not divide PP, nearest-integer conventions, changes of moduli or dimensions, adaptive side information, or simulations required only on a restricted secret-dependent support. The carry identity, finite prediction formula, and marginal-versus-joint distinction are standard tools; the specific affine classification and sharp examples constitute the contribution of this note.

8. Reproducible finite verification

The supplementary program scripts/verify.py uses integer arithmetic and fractions.Fraction. It checks all affine rows and translations for proper divisors Q≥2Q\ge2 of 4≤P≤124\le P\le12, in dimensions m=1,2m=1,2, and also m=3m=3 for P≤6P\le6. Direct constancy on the zero cell is compared with the stated normal form; the verifier finds a collision witness for every excluded affine row map. This covers 15,082 affine rows, 1,923,906 evaluated points, and 14,578 excluded maps for which a collision is found. The predicted number of admissible affine rows for a fixed domain is P+2mKP+2mK.

Further checks verify (2) in 1,451,375 signed arithmetic cases and verify the prediction and joint-distance formulas for 45 laws with 2≤Q≤162\le Q\le16 and K∈{2,3,4}K\in\{2,3,4\}. Reflection and restricted-support examples are also checked. The binary-secret proof is analytic; its additional probability-grid check is only a finite corroboration. Complete outputs, parameter domains, dependencies, and reproduction commands accompany the paper. These computations do not replace the proofs or establish any cryptographic security level.
