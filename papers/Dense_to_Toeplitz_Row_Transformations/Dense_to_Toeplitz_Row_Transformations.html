Dense and Toeplitz Matrices under Signed Row Permutations

Orbit constraints, optimal total variation, and exact rejection

Samir Bouftass and ChatGPT (OpenAI)

16 November 2026 · English edition 1.1

Abstract

We consider transformations that receive a uniform dense matrix over ℤP\mathbb Z_P and adaptively permute and negate its rows. For a uniform Toeplitz target of size m×nm\times n, the probability of reaching the target support is at most min(1,2m−1m!P−(m−1)(n−1))\min(1,2^{m-1}m!P^{-(m-1)(n-1)}). The exact minimum total variation is determined by the source and target masses of signed-row orbits; it can be strictly larger than the support obstruction. If rejection on a single source draw is permitted, the largest acceptance probability producing an exactly uniform Toeplitz matrix is P−(m−1)(n−1)P^{-(m-1)(n-1)}. The zero orbit alone imposes this bound. We also give the complete acceptance-versus-error formula and an explicit small rectangular example separating support success from distributional accuracy. These results apply classical counting, total variation, and capacity arguments to a restricted matrix transformation class. Exact enumeration of 274,688 matrices and independent transport linear programs corroborate the formulas. The results concern matrix marginals and do not establish a general obstruction to cryptographic reductions.

1. Model and motivation

Fix P=2pP=2^p with p≥2p\ge2, and m,n≥2m,n\ge2. Let Ω=ℤPm×n\Omega=\mathbb Z_P^{m\times n}, and let AA be uniform on Ω\Omega. A Toeplitz matrix has entries Tij=ti−jT_{ij}=t_{i-j} for 0≤i<m0\le i<m, 0≤j<n0\le j<n. Write 𝒯\mathcal T for this set and ν\nu for its uniform law. Its diagonals are free even over the composite ring ℤP\mathbb Z_P, so

N=|Ω|=Pmn,t=|𝒯|=Pm+n−1,d=(m−1)(n−1),N/t=Pd. N=|\Omega|=P^{mn},\qquad t=|\mathcal T|=P^{m+n-1},\qquad d=(m-1)(n-1),\qquad N/t=P^d. \tag{1}

Let GG be the group of signed permutations of the mm rows, of order 2mm!2^m m!. A converter sees the entire matrix AA, selects U∈GU\in G using an arbitrary distribution depending on AA, and outputs

B=UA. B=UA. \tag{2}

Thus the choice is adaptive, not independent of the source. There are no column operations, changes of dimensions or modulus, or rejection until rejection is explicitly introduced below. Optimization is information-theoretic: an optimal selection need not be efficient.

Two questions must be separated. Can the converter reach a Toeplitz matrix? Can it produce the uniform Toeplitz distribution? The latter requires correct masses inside the reachable support. Total variation, denoted Δ\Delta, is half the ℓ1\ell^1 distance and measures this distributional discrepancy [1].

1.1 A self-contained connection with rounding

Signed row permutations arise naturally when an affine operation must be computable from an already rounded vector for every input. The following proposition establishes this connection directly. The matrix theorems later in the paper only require the class (2).

Scalar cell lemma. Let P=LQP=LQ with L,Q≥2L,Q\ge2, and let a,b∈ℤPa,b\in\mathbb Z_P. The values [b+ar]P[b+ar]_P, 0≤r<Q0\le r<Q, all lie in one aligned cell Iv={Qv,…,Qv+Q−1}I_v=\{Qv,\ldots,Qv+Q-1\} if and only if a=0a=0; a=1a=1 and b≡0(modQ)b\equiv0\pmod Q; or a=−1a=-1 and b≡Q−1(modQ)b\equiv Q-1\pmod Q.

Proof. The case a=0a=0 is immediate. Otherwise, choose 1≤a<P1\le a<P, put g=gcd(a,P)g=\gcd(a,P), and let ℓ=P/g≥2\ell=P/g\ge2 be its additive order. If ℓ≤Q\ell\le Q, the progression includes its entire coset of the subgroup generated by aa. Its canonical representatives are

u,u+g,…,u+P−g,u=[b]g. u,u+g,\ldots,u+P-g,\qquad u=[b]_g.

Their diameter is P−g≥P/2≥QP-g\ge P/2\ge Q, whereas a cell has diameter Q−1Q-1. This is impossible. Thus ℓ>Q\ell>Q, and the QQ distinct progression values occupy all QQ points of IvI_v. Translating by aa removes bb and adds b+Qab+Qa modulo PP. All Q+1Q+1 progression values are distinct, so |Iv∩(Iv+a)|=Q−1|I_v\cap(I_v+a)|=Q-1.

Translation of the cell to {0,…,Q−1}\{0,\ldots,Q-1\} gives, by counting the nonwrapped and wrapped parts separately,

|Iv∩(Iv+a)|=max(0,Q−a)+max(0,Q−(P−a)). |I_v\cap(I_v+a)|=\max(0,Q-a)+\max(0,Q-(P-a)).

Because 2Q≤P2Q\le P, the two terms cannot both be positive. Their sum equals Q−1>0Q-1>0 only when a=1a=1 or a=P−1a=P-1. For a=1a=1, the progression starts at the unique cell point whose predecessor is outside the cell, so b=Qvb=Qv modulo PP. For a=−1a=-1, it starts at the unique cell point whose successor is outside, so b=Qv+Q−1b=Qv+Q-1. Both choices plainly suffice. In particular, for Q=2Q=2, the overlap must equal one, which gives the same two steps; a step of order two was already excluded by the diameter argument. ▫\square

Proposition 1. Let P=LQP=LQ with L,Q≥2L,Q\ge2, and let F(z)=⌊[z]P/Q⌋F(z)=\lfloor[z]_P/Q\rfloor act coordinatewise. If UU is square and invertible over ℤP\mathbb Z_P, then F(Uz+b)F(Uz+b) is a function of F(z)F(z) for every zz if and only if UU is a signed permutation and each positive row has bi≡0(modQ)b_i\equiv0\pmod Q, while each negative row has bi≡Q−1(modQ)b_i\equiv Q-1\pmod Q. Without a translation, only ordinary permutations are possible.

Proof. Writing z=Qy+rz=Qy+r with 0≤rj<Q0\le r_j<Q gives

F(Uz+b)=[Uy+⌊Ur+bQ⌋]L. F(Uz+b)=\left[Uy+\left\lfloor\frac{Ur+b}{Q}\right\rfloor\right]_L. \tag{3}

This identity uses arbitrary integer representatives of the coefficients. Changing a lift by PP changes the quotient correction by a multiple of LL, which the outer reduction removes. It is necessary and sufficient that the remainder correction be constant on the zero cell.

Fix a row ii. Set all remainders to zero except rjr_j, for a nonzero coefficient UijU_{ij}. The scalar cell lemma forces this coefficient to be +1+1 with bi≡0(modQ)b_i\equiv0\pmod Q, or −1-1 with bi≡Q−1(modQ)b_i\equiv Q-1\pmod Q. A row cannot contain both signs: these two congruences on the same bib_i are incompatible since Q≥2Q\ge2.

If a row contains two positive coefficients, choose their remainders equal to Q−1Q-1 and every other remainder zero. Relative to the zero vector, the quotient correction increases by ⌊2(Q−1)/Q⌋=1\lfloor2(Q-1)/Q\rfloor=1. For two negative coefficients, write bi=Qβ+Q−1b_i=Q\beta+Q-1 and choose their integer representatives as −1-1. The same choice of remainders changes the correction from β\beta to β−1\beta-1, because −(Q−1)/Q-(Q-1)/Q lies strictly between −1-1 and zero. Neither change vanishes modulo L≥2L\ge2. These calculations include Q=2Q=2.

Thus each row has at most one nonzero coefficient. Invertibility excludes zero rows and repeated selected columns: with a repeated selected column, a square matrix of this form leaves another column zero. Hence UU is a signed permutation. Conversely, the scalar cell lemma and (3) show that every listed row gives an output determined by yy. Finally, when b=0b=0, the negative-row congruence is impossible for Q≥2Q\ge2. ▫\square

For a matrix-dependent choice, the universal premise must hold for every realized choice. Restricting it to a particular secret-dependent support would be a different condition. Furthermore, applying a permissible transformation to Y=F(As+e)Y=F(As+e) gives F(UAs+Ue+b)F(UAs+Ue+b). A negative row’s required translation may change the target noise law. Symmetry of the original noise does not remove that translation.

1.2 Matrix marginals and full observations

A converter receiving (A,Y)(A,Y) may select UU using both inputs. Averaging its selection over YY conditional on AA still gives a matrix kernel of the form (2). Therefore every marginal obstruction below remains necessary for such a converter. Attaining a marginal optimum does not establish that it is attainable together with a prescribed rounded output or secret correlation.

2. The support obstruction

Define G𝒯={UT:U∈G,T∈𝒯}G\mathcal T=\{UT:U\in G,T\in\mathcal T\} and

s*=|G𝒯|N,h=2m−1m!. s_* =\frac{|G\mathcal T|}{N},\qquad h=2^{m-1}m!.

Theorem 1. The largest probability of obtaining a Toeplitz matrix by (2) is s*s_*. Moreover,

s*≤min(1,hP−d),Δ(ℒ(B),ν)≥1−s*≥max(0,1−hP−d). s_*\le\min(1,hP^{-d}),\qquad \Delta(\mathcal L(B),\nu)\ge1-s_*\ge\max(0,1-hP^{-d}). \tag{4}

Proof. An input can be sent into 𝒯\mathcal T exactly when its orbit meets 𝒯\mathcal T, or equivalently when it belongs to G𝒯G\mathcal T. Choosing a witness on that set attains the claimed probability. This argument already allows the choice to depend on the whole input.

Global negation preserves 𝒯\mathcal T, so U𝒯=(−U)𝒯U\mathcal T=(-U)\mathcal T. The group elements pair into hh pairs because P≥4P\ge4. The union therefore has at most htht elements. Finally, the target is always Toeplitz, whereas the transformed source is Toeplitz with probability at most s*s_*. Testing this event gives (4). ▫\square

Toeplitz membership is checked by comparing adjacent diagonal entries in O(mn)O(mn) operations. The obstruction therefore gives an efficient test even when orbit enumeration is expensive. The union bound need not be tight. For P=4P=4 and m=n=3m=n=3, it gives s*≤3/32s_*\le3/32, improving the unpaired group count by a factor of two.

4. Computing the signed-row orbits

Replace each row rr by the lexicographically smaller of rr and −r-r, and sort the resulting rows. This multiset is a complete invariant. If distinct row types have multiplicities c1,…,cjc_1,\ldots,c_j, and r0r_0 counts all row occurrences whose type is not fixed by negation, then

|O|=m!∏ici!2r0. |O|=\frac{m!}{\prod_i c_i!}\,2^{r_0}. \tag{7}

The multinomial factor places the canonical row types into the mm labelled positions, accounting for repeated types through the factors ci!c_i!. Once these positions are fixed, every occurrence of a type not fixed by negation has two distinct orientations, chosen independently at that position. This remains true for repeated types: the exponent counts occurrences, not distinct types. A type fixed by negation has only one orientation at each occurrence; its entries all belong to {0,P/2}\{0,P/2\}. Every resulting matrix determines its row-type placement and orientations uniquely, so there is no additional overcount. Ignoring fixed rows or repeated types would incorrectly assume a free action.

Enumerating the Pm+n−1P^{m+n-1} diagonal sequences gives |O∩𝒯||O\cap\mathcal T|. Together with (7), this yields (4)–(6) without enumerating every dense matrix. The supplement also performs the larger dense enumeration as an independent finite check. Neither procedure is claimed to be polynomial in m,n,logPm,n,\log P.

5. Rejection on one source draw

We now allow the converter to return either a matrix in GAGA or a rejection symbol. Let α>0\alpha>0 be its acceptance probability, and require the accepted output to follow ν\nu exactly.

Theorem 2. The maximum exact acceptance probability is

αexact=P−d. \alpha_{\mathrm{exact}}=P^{-d}. \tag{8}

Proof. The accepted masses must satisfy αqO≤pO\alpha q_O\le p_O for every orbit. The zero matrix is a singleton orbit with source mass 1/N1/N and target mass 1/t1/t. Hence α/t≤1/N\alpha/t\le1/N, giving α≤P−d\alpha\le P^{-d}. Accepting precisely when A∈𝒯A\in\mathcal T and returning AA attains the bound. ▫\square

The zero matrix is rare but cannot be discarded under an exact distributional requirement. Its argument uses only the implication UA=0⇒A=0UA=0\Rightarrow A=0, so the same exact acceptance upper bound holds if arbitrary invertible row operations are allowed. Repeating the identity filter on independent new draws takes PdP^d draws on average. This statement presupposes access to those draws and does not describe a general adaptive multiple-instance reduction.

7. Boundaries of the transformation class

Column changes matter. Modulo 88, take the rows of AA to be (1,0)(1,0) and (2,3)(2,3). No signed row permutation is Toeplitz: without exchanging rows one would need 1=±31=\pm3, and with an exchange one would need 2=±02=\pm0. Yet A2=IA^2=I, so allowing the column transformation V=AV=A gives the Toeplitz matrix AV=IAV=I. This refutes an extension of the support obstruction to arbitrary column operations. It does not produce a uniform Toeplitz matrix.

A reflection needs its translation. For P=4,Q=2P=4,Q=2, the values 00 and 11 have the same quotient but their negatives do not. The corrected reflection x↦−x+1x\mapsto-x+1 satisfies F(−x+1)=[−F(x)]2F(-x+1)=[-F(x)]_2.

Toeplitz and Hankel have the same orbit saturation. The matrix with rows (0,1)(0,1) and (2,0)(2,0) is Toeplitz but not Hankel, so the supports are different. Their conversion problems under (2), however, are equivalent. Let ℋ\mathcal H be the Hankel support and let R∈GR\in G reverse the row order. If Tij=ti−jT_{ij}=t_{i-j}, then

(RT)ij=tm−1−(i+j). (RT)_{ij}=t_{m-1-(i+j)}.

This depends only on i+ji+j, and the inverse row reversal sends every Hankel matrix to a Toeplitz matrix. Thus R𝒯=ℋR\mathcal T=\mathcal H. Since R2=IR^2=I and RR acts within each GG-orbit,

Gℋ=G𝒯,|O∩ℋ|=|O∩𝒯|for every orbit O. G\mathcal H=G\mathcal T,\qquad |O\cap\mathcal H|=|O\cap\mathcal T|\quad\text{for every orbit }O.

The two target sets also have equal size. Consequently, their uniform target masses qOq_O agree orbit by orbit. They have the same optimal support success s*s_*, minimum distance δ*\delta_*, exact acceptance threshold, and entire curve δ(α)\delta(\alpha) in this matrix conversion model. Column reversal also exchanges the supports but lies outside (2); row reversal already supplies the equivalence using an allowed operation.

For a source (A,F(As+e))(A,F(As+e)) and a target whose public matrix is always Toeplitz, the error of the full view is at least the error of its matrix marginal. Our bounds are therefore necessary whenever the output matrix remains in GAGA. Conversely, an optimal matrix kernel need not preserve the observation or its secret dependence. Conditioning solely on A∈𝒯A\in\mathcal T preserves independent secret and noise laws, but it incurs the acceptance cost in (8).

8. Verification and relation to prior work

The exact verifier uses integers and fractions.Fraction to examine 274,688 dense matrices in the five displayed domains. It compares canonical orbit counting with a direct search for a signed ordering whose row prefixes and suffixes match. The latter does not use the canonical representative to decide reachability. Every enumerated orbit size is checked against (7), and complete target-orbit profiles and tradeoff values are supplied.

A separate transport program builds all reachable destinations directly at matrix level, without importing the orbit implementation. It solves six linear programs: three acceptance rates each for (P,m,n)=(4,2,2)(P,m,n)=(4,2,2) and (4,3,2)(4,3,2). SciPy/HiGHS results are compared with the exact fractions at tolerance 10−810^{-8}. Floating-point optimization corroborates but does not certify the general formulas. Negative controls implement the three examples in Section 7. An additional standard-library check directly tests the scalar cell lemma, affine rows and small invertible matrices, and checks the Toeplitz/Hankel target-orbit correspondence and the orbit-size formula by explicit group images. Its exact domains and counters are recorded in data/structure_results.json; finite checks corroborate the proofs rather than extending their hypotheses.

The passage to partition masses and common-mass maximization are standard total-variation tools [1]. Budgeted rejection for general divergences is studied by Verine et al. [2]; (9) is the elementary finite capacity form needed here. Block and Polyanskiy [3] instead study selection among several independent draws, with bounds in terms of the number of samples. Their broader sampling model is not excluded by our one-draw conditional argument.

The contribution of this note is the explicit diagnosis of signed-row conversion to the Toeplitz distribution: the paired support count, exact orbit formulas, the rectangular excess-mass example, and the zero-orbit acceptance threshold. It does not establish impossibility for transformations that change columns, secrets, moduli, dimensions, supports, or the number of instances. No hardness reduction or security claim follows from these matrix calculations alone.
